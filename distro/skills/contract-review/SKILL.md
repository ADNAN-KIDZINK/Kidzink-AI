# Contract review

Start every review with one line: this is a summary to help them read the contract, not legal advice; anything important should be checked with the legal or finance team.

## Read it all first
- Use the pdf-files or word-documents skill to read the full text. Note clause or page numbers as you go.

## Key terms table
Give a table with: Topic | What the contract says | Clause/page. Cover what's present among:
parties; what's being bought or provided; price, payment terms and currency; duration and renewal (watch for automatic renewal); delivery and deadlines; warranties; liability and caps; penalties and late fees; termination (who can end it, notice, costs); confidentiality; intellectual property (who owns designs and drawings; this matters for Kidzink's design work); data protection; governing law and courts.

## Risks and points to raise
- List the clauses that are unusual, one-sided or risky for Kidzink, most serious first, each with why it matters in plain words and a suggested question or change to ask for.
- Point out anything missing that you'd normally expect (no liability cap, no termination right, no delivery date).

## Rules
- Quote exact wording for anything important. Never paraphrase a number, date or obligation loosely.
- If the document is unclear or contradicts itself, say so; don't resolve it by guessing.
- Don't draft legal language as if it were final. You can suggest wording for the person to discuss with legal.
