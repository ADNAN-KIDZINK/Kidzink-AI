# Brainstorming

## Approach
1. Make sure you know the goal, the audience and any constraints (budget, timing, brand). Ask one short question if one of these is missing and it matters.
2. Give a wide first round: 8–12 ideas that are genuinely different from each other, from safe to bold. One line each.
3. Group them if there are clear themes.
4. Pick your top three, each with why it works and one risk or open question.
5. Offer to develop any of them further.

## Rules
- Fit Kidzink: learning spaces, school design and educational furniture, for schools and students around the world. Keep ideas practical for that context unless the person wants blue-sky thinking.
- Quantity first, then judgement. Don't dismiss ideas in the first round.
- Build on the person's ideas when they share some; say what you'd change.
- No invented facts, statistics or claims about competitors.
- For names and slogans, mention that availability and trademarks need checking.
